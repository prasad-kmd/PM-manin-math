# Scene Structure and Explanatory Flow

## General pattern

Use this sequence as a guide, not a mandatory template for every video.

1. **Question or motivation:** establish the mathematical problem or surprising idea.
2. **Intuition:** show a simple visual, example, or relationship that makes the question concrete.
3. **Development:** build the key construction or derive the result step by step.
4. **Connection:** explain why the construction, equation, or transformation matters.
5. **Conclusion:** state the result or central insight clearly.

Short videos may combine stages. A proof-focused video may need a different structure.

## Opening

- Start with the mathematical question or central visual.
- Avoid long title sequences and decorative introductions.
- Do not display a title card if it delays the explanation without adding context.
- Introduce notation before relying on it.

## Development

- Introduce one main idea at a time.
- Build diagrams and equations in a logical order.
- Use pauses after difficult steps.
- Reuse the same object when the explanation depends on continuity.
- Keep supporting text concise and directly related to the current step.

## Conclusion

- Leave the final equation, diagram, or insight on-screen long enough to read.
- Ensure the conclusion follows from the preceding explanation.
- Avoid adding a new concept in the final moments unless the video is explicitly designed to introduce it.
- Do not add a forced call to action or branded end card by default.

## Scene transitions

- Prefer transitions that preserve conceptual continuity.
- Fade or remove objects when they are no longer relevant.
- Avoid abrupt scene changes that make the mathematical state difficult to follow.
- Keep persistent elements, including the watermark, consistent without allowing them to clutter the scene.
